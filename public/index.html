2 · Cookie pool — build warm profiles in advance

Builds empty Chrome profiles with nobody signed in and browses normally in each one (Google, YouTube, News, real sites, searches) until it holds the cookies a genuine browser accumulates. They wait in the pool until an account needs one. Building trust before a login exists is what keeps reCAPTCHA away — a brand new profile whose first act is signing into Google is exactly what gets challenged.

3 · Re-warm cookies — refresh every existing profile

Cookies decay. NID and AEC expire, SOCS gets cleared, a profile sits unused for weeks, or an import rebuilt the cookie store and only the login came back. The profile still looks long-lived on disk — it just stopped carrying the cookies that tell Google it has been seen before, and the next search from it is the one that gets challenged.

This browses normally in every profile and keeps going until the jar actually grades healthy, then re-opens each profile offline to confirm the cookies survived the browser closing. Signed-in profiles stay signed in — warming only browses, it never signs anything out.

Roughly a minute per profile, plus a pause between each — warming them back to back from one proxy pool is its own pattern.

4 · New accounts — provision

Every account above that has no profile yet gets one: first from the warm pool, and only when the pool is empty does it build a new profile, warm it up, and then sign in. Existing accounts are left alone — if they are signed out, that is button 1.

Move profiles to another laptop

Chrome encrypts cookies with a key tied to this Windows account and machine, so simply copying chrome-profiles/ arrives logged out — which is what triggers the CAPTCHAs. Export reads the cookies out while they are still readable; Import writes them back on the new machine.

Step 1 · On this laptop (source)

Zips the whole folder correctly — skips ~7 GB of rebuildable cache, keeps .env, chrome-profiles/ and node_modules/. Written next to the bot folder.

Step 2 · On the other laptop (target)

Unzip, run npm start, then click Import here. It strips the old machine's encryption key, clears stale locks and caches, re-injects every cookie, and re-opens each profile to confirm the cookies stuck.
